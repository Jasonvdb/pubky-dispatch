# Account creation bindings

This document is normative. It specifies signer request authority across account creation and erasure (spec 4.3, 6.1 and 12.7; wire 3.7 and 7.1-7.4). Account creation time identifies one lifetime at one service; an identity key or service key alone does not. All encodings and signature verification use wire 1 and 2.

## 1. Existing-account requests

A signer request concerning an existing account carries exactly one `Switch-Created` header: its creation time in the wire 1.6 form with exactly three fractional digits. The header is required on every signer call except `GET /v1/{id}/account` and the `create` enrollment of Section 3. It is absent on those two calls, on app calls, and on a next service's export request. A disallowed, missing, malformed, repeated, empty or non-printable value is `400 bad_request`. The public identity read remains header-independent as wire 7.4.15 specifies.

Enrollment applies Section 3's locked intent and receipt checks. Its exact completed-receipt retry may retain a takeover intent's old creation while returning the new creation that the same intent already established; it cannot apply that intent again. Every other existing-account signer call uses the common comparison below.

The ordinary request signature appends the exact `Switch-Created` text, or an empty field when absent, after `Switch-Invite-Code`:

```
lp("pubky-switch/v1/http", method, path and query, hash(body),
   Switch-Timestamp, origin, Switch-Identity, Switch-App, Switch-Cert,
   Switch-Invite-Code, Switch-Created)
```

The response's received-request representation appends `H(Switch-Created)` after `H(Switch-Signature)`. `H` is wire 7.1's presence-preserving encoding: absent is empty, present is `lp` of every raw value in received order. All eight protected headers obey the same raw-header rule. The next service's `transfer-request` signature input is unchanged; its received-request creation field is absent. The public identity read binds all eight headers as absent.

A sender records the creation binding with the pending operation before the first send. Refreshing `Switch-Timestamp` preserves that binding and the exact signed body. A restore, hand-over, authenticated conflict or discovery of another creation cannot silently retarget a pending operation. The signer resolves that operation's outcome or obtains authorization for a distinct action; neither a missing receipt nor current absence proves that an earlier call did not commit. Section 3 specifies when an explicit recovery authorization may end an enrollment-only retry continuation without resolving its remote outcome.

Under the identity lock, after authentication and a fresh judgement of the account, mark, erasure outcome and store identity, the service compares the binding with the selected creation of Section 1.1, before applying wire 7.3's effective account verdict. A mismatch is `409 conflict`, with no `Switch-Head` and `current` an object of exactly one member, `created`: the current creation as a valid `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.sssZ` string (wire 1.6). This common mismatch has the same shape whether the account survives or only its mark does. Its response time is outside the comparison of the requested account lifetime's committed times (wire 7.1), and the check does not stamp an account time. A known absent account remains `404 not_found` with `current.hosted` false. Unknown store or erasure outcome remains `503 unavailable`. No request-dependent stamping, sweep, enrollment, key/mark update, last-seen update or endpoint mutation precedes this check. A request queued behind erasure is judged again after it obtains the lock. The ordinary final judgement and durable-answer rules still apply.

This comparison precedes state-dependent success and the authenticated failing/bounded account's endpoint handling. An unauthenticated caller still receives only what wire 7.3 Table 2 permits. A matching binding grants no endpoint permission or exception to the recovery, retirement, frozen, staged or erasure checks. An ordinary call with a matching creation keeps wire 7.3's account verdict: a lost account receives the existing recovery notice, while a non-retired (0, 0) mark without its account remains not hosted and returns `404 not_found` with `current.hosted` false. Enrollment and erasure retain their specified handling. An erasure's body `created` must equal its header; a mismatch is `400`. Its document comparison and authenticated `409` contents otherwise remain wire 7.4.16's. A signer without the binding uses Section 2 rather than an intentionally incomplete destructive request.

### 1.1 Account and mark association

Judge both the account and the mark as read, under the identity lock, before the ordinary account tables or an endpoint uses either. Completed-erasure cleanup, unreadable/changing store identity, an account that cannot be read and unresolved erasure outcomes retain their existing precedence. Discovery performs no cleanup. Retain both readable inputs and their bounds. Where the table below treats one of them as missing, the account or mark as read is still kept.

| Readable inputs after those checks | Selected creation and association | Effective account judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Account and mark with equal creation | that creation, `account` | unchanged wire 7.3 Tables 1-3 |
| Account, no mark or a record that is not a readable mark | the account's creation, `account` | `mark_lost`, with the existing retirement and response-key rules |
| No account, surviving readable mark | the mark's creation, `mark` | lost account; a non-retired zero mark retains ordinary not-hosted behavior |
| Account created before the mark | the mark's creation, `mark` | current lifetime has no associated account; the older account cannot serve or authenticate as its state |
| Account created after the mark | the account's creation, `account` | current lifetime has no associated mark; `mark_lost`, even if the older mark's pair/hash matches the account's state |
| No account and an established missing mark | null, `absent` | not hosted |

An unreadable store, unresolved outcome or otherwise unestablished input gives no usable association. An older readable mark is not literally a missing mark: its exact record, generation and retirement bounds are retained for Section 3.2. With `account` association and an older mark, the account's document supplies the lost-mark response-key rule; the old mark does not select the key. With `mark` association, the mark supplies the current lifetime's key and lost-account rules; the older account's document is not a current copy.

A signer making a distinct enrollment under its explicit recovery authorization first obtains this signed association and durably selects one intent: `account` selects `existing`, `mark` selects `takeover`, and `absent` selects `create`. An error never changes that selection. An existing exact pending enrollment remains its own continuation until Section 3's explicit authorization ends it; this observation does not resolve its unknown outcome.

An erasure binds the selected later creation. With an account created before its mark, apply the lost-account document rule, with no current document borrowed from the older account, and permit the authorized erasure even when the selected mark is zero. With an account created after its mark, use that account's document and normal lost-mark erasure restrictions. An older-bound request cannot delete the newer lifetime. Whenever an account is present, including a superseded one, its deletion completes only on durable evidence that the deletion committed. That no account is associated with the selected lifetime cannot authorize the completion used when no account is present. Publish completed erasure only after the exact deletion outcome is established, at a time covering the selected creation and applicable time floor; only then remove covered marks. Unknown outcome precedence is unchanged.

## 2. Authenticated account discovery

`GET /v1/{id}/account` is a signer-only read. It has no query or body. It requires the canonical identity in the path and `Switch-Identity`, one `Switch-Timestamp` and one `Switch-Signature`; `Switch-App`, `Switch-Cert`, `Switch-Invite-Code`, `Switch-Transfer` and `Switch-Created` are absent. A caller other than the identity is refused by wire 7.2. Signature, timestamp, source limits and authenticated caller limits apply, including when the identity is not hosted. The request authenticates with the identity key independently of a stored account.

The service takes the identity lock and reads the current account, mark and completed-erasure record from the same confirmed store identity. It returns a `200` observation at staged, healthy, bounded, failing or lost accounts when those sources establish the creation; it may also observe known absence. It signs no head and performs no account creation, enrollment, delivery, admission completion, hand-over, stamping, sweep or mark change. If a covered restored account or leftover mark needs deletion before a consistent read is possible, it returns `503 unavailable`; the existing erasure cleanup completes separately. An unresolved erasure attempt is unavailable, never absence. A store identity that cannot be read or changes during the observation is also unavailable.

After a rebuilt store's new identity and current records are coherently confirmed, a later discovery may describe that store's current state under these rules. The replacement inherits no observation or receipt authority from its predecessor, does not refresh or rebind a pending action, and does not resolve an unknown prior outcome. Its new identity is not proof that an identity never had an account or that a pending request did not commit. Surviving accounts whose marks were lost still follow wire 7.3's lost-mark verdict and recovery rules. A fresh observation may support a creation or mark takeover only through a distinct authorized intent satisfying Section 3.

The observation reports Section 1.1's selected creation and association. A surviving mark, including a zero mark or one beside a proven-older account, is not absence. `created` is null exactly when `association` is `absent`; `account` and `mark` require the selected creation. The two readable creation disagreements of Section 1.1 are established states, not contradictory observations. Other contradictory or unestablished state remains unavailable. No account plus a malformed mark record is not established absence. An absence observation describes only this store's current state, not the identity's history.

The successful body is at most 1,024 exact bytes, including JSON whitespace and escapes. The service bounds those bytes before signing; the HTTP reader bounds response accumulation, and the shared reader refuses oversized input before allocating parsed fields. Oversized discovery is unusable evidence, never absence. The body is one strict JSON object. Every field below is present; unknown members, omitted fields and wrong null forms are malformed. Times have exactly three fractional digits.

| Field | Type | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `v` | int | `1` |
| `kind` | string | `pubky-switch.account` |
| `identity` | pubky | the identity in the request |
| `origin` | origin | the exact request-signature origin |
| `service_key` | `b64(32)` | the current service key signing this response |
| `store_id` | `b64(16)` | the existing mark-store identity's 16 bytes; not a new per-request value |
| `association` | string | exactly `account`, `mark` or `absent`, from Section 1.1 |
| `created` | time or `null` | the selected creation; null exactly for `absent` |
| `erased_at` | time or `null` | the latest completed erasure record, or its established absence |
| `observed_at` | time | the trusted time used for this locked observation |
| `expires_at` | time | exactly `observed_at + 300000` milliseconds |

The existing response signature authenticates the observation. Status is `200`, `Switch-Head` is absent and `Switch-Server-Time` equals the exact `observed_at` text. This call's times are outside comparison of an account's committed service times, like enrollment. Every answer to this call is headless. An error is not an observation.

A signer checks the response against the exact request it sent before retaining any field. At first use of an explicitly selected service origin it verifies with the body's `service_key`, tied to the origin by the checked HTTPS connection, as for an enrollment. With a pin it requires that pin, except within the user-authorized provider-loss or service-key-change rules already prescribed by spec 12.5-12.6. An observation does not itself change an existing pin, start recovery, establish the current identity document or authorize a pending operation at a different creation. An unpinned signer may make this read; other unpinned calls remain restricted by wire 7.1.

### 2.1 Carrying the exact signed observation

An enrollment carries the observation as this strict object, with all four fields required:

| Field | Type | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `body` | base64url | the exact successful discovery response body bytes |
| `sig` | `b64(64)` | its `Switch-Service-Signature` |
| `request_timestamp` | time | the exact original `Switch-Timestamp` text, with no fractional digits |
| `request_signature` | `b64(64)` | the exact original `Switch-Signature` value |

This is a carried HTTP response, not a signed document under another label. To verify it, decode and strictly read `body`, reconstruct `GET /v1/{identity}/account` without a query, empty request body, the body's origin, `Switch-Identity` from the body's identity, the two carried request headers and every other protected header absent. Verify the identity's original request signature over Section 1's input. Build wire 7.1's received request for that exact request, with `hash(empty)` as body field. Verify `sig` with the body's service key over:

```
lp("pubky-switch/v1/http-response", u64(200), hash(received request),
   observed_at text, empty, hash(body bytes))
```

No reserialization, omitted original request field, arbitrary received-request hash, early unread-body representation, other route/status or added head is accepted. The original request timestamp is authenticated context; it is not rechecked as the enrollment wrapper's current timestamp. Section 2.2 controls the observation's validity. The carried body, signature and original request fields are retained byte for byte with the enrollment intent. The reader checks the base64url body's encoded length before allocating decoded bytes and requires at most 1,024 decoded body bytes. No reader removes whitespace or reserializes those signed bytes to fit the limit.

The signer constructs the unsigned carrying object and initial intent locally in compact JSON from those exact fields; an external raw carrying-object encoding is not a required future-enrollment input. No separate carrying-object cap applies. Under wire 7.1's 267-byte origin limit, the maximum compact observation has 659 bytes, including all eleven required fields, `association: "account"` and four non-null millisecond times. The 1,024-byte exact-body cap leaves 365 bytes for optional whitespace/escapes. Its base64url text is at most 1,366 characters; with the two 86-character signatures, 20-character request timestamp and object syntax, the compact carrier is at most 1,624 bytes. The compact Create intent is at most 1,656 bytes and Takeover, with its creation, 1,695 bytes.

Wire 3.7's future-enrollment size guarantee reserves that largest intent and the longest future origin, in addition to its existing document, revoked-app and signing headroom accounting. For B decoded enrollment-body bytes, the compact signed HTTP request has `121 + ceil(8 * B / 6)` bytes, including its outer enrollment member, signed wrapper and signature; base64 rounding is part of the bound. Every actual complete enrollment must fit 256 KiB before it is signed or sent, counting any chosen outer whitespace and escapes. An already signed pending enrollment keeps its complete original bytes; the size rule never permits canonicalizing or changing its signed body on retry.

### 2.2 Trusted validity and retention

An observation is issued only with usable trusted time as defined by wire 7.3: the authenticated sample plus elapsed monotonic time, within the maximum sample age and uncertainty. Use that trusted time itself for `observed_at` and validity, not the wall clock, the request timestamp or a possibly future account-stamped time. An unrepresentable expiry, or a relevant creation or erasure time ahead of that trusted time, is `503 unavailable`; no fresh-creation authority is minted. Missing/unusable trusted time cannot be replaced by the ordinary request timestamp fallback.

For an action that creates an account, including a mark takeover, the locked check requires usable trusted time `now` and:

```
observed_at <= now < expires_at
expires_at = observed_at + 300000 ms
```

At the exact expiry the observation is expired. There is no extra grace, including the HTTP timestamp tolerance. A future observation cannot authorize creation; while trusted time is behind it the answer is `503 unavailable`. An expired observation is `409 conflict` with `current: { "binding": "expired" }`. A malformed expiry is `400 bad_request`. A still-valid observation authorizes nothing unless its exact identity, origin, service key, store identity and association/erasure/creation tuple also pass Section 3. Future or unusable trusted time never grants authority by selecting a different time base.

The five-minute bound is strictly below the existing one-day erasure-record retention floor. Even allowing two authenticated samples at opposite ends of the permitted five-minute uncertainty, `300000 + 2 * 300000 < 86400000` milliseconds. Thus an observation from before a completed erasure expires before that erasure can be collected; while the record remains, its changed exact binding rejects the observation. Collection still requires the trusted-time and leftover-cleanup rules of wire 7.3 Table 3. A caller-selected future intent time cannot extend this service-authenticated expiry. An old absence cannot become fresh authority when a record is collected or the store is replaced.

## 3. Enrollment intents and locked decisions

The signed Enrollment body of wire 3.7 gains one required strict `intent` object. The object's `kind` selects exactly one form; other members, mixed forms and omitted required fields are `400 bad_request`:

| `kind` | Other members | Request creation header | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `create` | `observation` of Section 2.1 | absent | create from an authenticated `absent` observation (`created: null`) |
| `existing` | `created`: time | exactly that time | enroll at that same existing account; never create an account |
| `takeover` | `created`: time, `observation` of Section 2.1 | exactly that time | explicitly authorize mark takeover; observation association is `mark` and `created` is that mark creation |

`created` uses exactly three fractional digits. The enrollment's identity and origin and any carried observation's identity and origin agree with the actual request. For a new creation or takeover in steps 3-4 below, a carried observation is signed by the current service key and names this existing mark-store identity; a different key/store/association/tuple is `409 conflict` with `current: { "binding": "changed" }`, not a new binding to adopt automatically. Cryptographically invalid evidence is `401 bad_signature`. Unreadable or changing store identity and an unresolved erasure outcome are `503 unavailable`, before a receipt or absence is used.

The service authenticates the request and signed enrollment, takes the identity lock, and rejudges current durable state before any request-dependent timestamp, mark write, account insertion, remembered revoked-app set or answer receipt. It then applies this order:

1. **An exact retained receipt at the same account.** A receipt associates the hash of the exact signed enrollment bytes with the creation and store identity where it was applied, and with its application result. Return that result only if the receipt's account is the one selected with `account` association, its creation equals that account's creation, its associated store identity is the currently confirmed one and the durable association is established. A lost or unassociated mark does not by itself invalidate a same-account receipt; a receipt held by a superseded older account never supplies a result for the selected marked lifetime. This is a retry, not creation or another application of `revoked_apps`. An expired observation does not invalidate this receipt. The original observation need not match a newly collected erasure record or a later service key merely to read a completed result, but the account must not be covered by an erasure or an unknown outcome. The existing service-key-change answer rules may name the current key; they never change the receipt's account association or reapply the intent.
2. **`existing`.** Require live `account` association and a matching account creation under Section 1; the presence of an account alone is insufficient. Apply only wire 3.7's existing-account behavior and keep its exact receipt association. With `mark` association, do not use the older account or enter either creation branch: return the effective lost-account notice, or the existing not-hosted answer for a non-retired zero mark. Known absence is `404`, and an unknown verdict is unavailable. A matching old mark alone does not authorize takeover.
3. **`create`.** Require a valid unexpired `absent` observation, the same live association and an equal current completed-erasure binding, with neither an account nor a surviving mark. Only then use wire 7.3 Table 3's new-account procedure. Choose creation strictly after every applicable erased creation as already required. If an account or zero mark is now present without the exact receipt of step 1, answer `409 conflict` with `current: { "binding": "changed" }`; do not assume that it belongs to this intent or that the earlier send failed.
4. **`takeover`.** Require a valid unexpired `mark` observation, the same live association, the exact selected mark creation and current store/erasure binding, and that readable surviving mark, not a completed-erasure leftover. The account must be absent or proven created before that mark; an account created after it is never replaced by this intent. Retain the mark's generation, sequence, chain hash and retirements, and choose a new creation strictly above both known creations and the completed-erasure floor. With an older account, Section 3.1 additionally preserves its safety baseline. A zero mark also requires this explicit bound action; it is not fresh absence. Changed association, creation, store or erasure evidence gives the binding conflict. No failing/bounded account or lost mark is converted into absence.

Fresh creation and takeover retain the enrollment result atomically with the account state to which it applies, before success. Existing mark-before-account and erasure-outcome ordering is unchanged. A failure, lost answer or unknown commit is resolved using the exact retained association and existing outcome rules. Neither a zero mark alone, an absent account, expired evidence nor an evicted receipt proves non-application. Receipt eviction removes the ability to return that exact result; it never authorizes the old intent at a replacement account. No challenge, nonce ledger or new durable store is introduced.

The signer durably retains the complete signed enrollment, its selected intent, original observation where present, target origin/key and any returned creation/result before dependent delivery. Wrapper timestamp refresh may not replace the observation, change intent kind, creation or body, or infer a new intent from an error. After expiry or changed state, resolving the old outcome and authorizing a distinct enrollment are separate actions. Discovery may establish the current creation after restore or hand-over, but cannot associate an old unresolved enrollment with it by itself. A recovery with the identity key alone (spec 12.8), which has no usable backup and so no original target, binds the creation its own authenticated discovery shows at the service the newest copy names, and enrolls there with `existing`. A new authorized recovery may select `takeover` when the current mark justifies it; it does not assert that the old send failed.

While an enrollment remains eligible for continuation, retries retain its exact signed bytes and binding. The user may explicitly authorize a distinct provider-loss recovery (spec 12.6) or the first-enrollment restart below to end that continuation while its remote outcome remains unknown. The user accepts that the earlier enrollment may have committed; ending continuation neither cancels it remotely nor declares failure or non-commit. Once ended, that enrollment cannot be retried or rebound, and a late answer cannot advance the replacement action. A resume, background retry, error, expiry or missing receipt never makes this choice. Retention for an active continuation does not require a permanent archive of enrollments whose continuations were explicitly ended.

This choice leaves all already signed documents and their version, generation, removal and revocation obligations intact, with the existing copy, trust and ownership checks and hand-over readiness. It does not end delivery or erasure uncertainty or change their rules.

If first discovery already selects an account or a mark before the install signs a `create`, first setup stops there. The install retains the authenticated observation across restart. The holder can use the confirmed signer restore in spec 12.8. If the Homeserver has no signed copy, the origin selected for setup can locate a serving account for the key-only survey. That survey still requires the service's verified identity document and the holder's confirmation. A surviving mark alone requires the applicable provider-loss procedure. An unanswered or unverifiable discovery leaves the initial discovery pending.

**Restarting first enrollment at the same service.** The holder of the identity key can restart setup from the original installation's intact first-enrollment records. The user confirms the same origin and accepts that the earlier creation may have committed. The signer first retries the exact saved `create`. If it receives the original receipt under step 1, it continues that enrollment, including step 1's service-key-change rule. Only a request-bound `409 conflict` authenticated by the originally observed key with exactly `current: { "binding": "expired" }` permits selecting a replacement. Every other error leaves the original enrollment pending.

The original and fresh authenticated observations must both show `absent` for the same identity, origin, service key and store, with no completed erasure. The install must still be awaiting that first enrollment, have spent no document version, and find neither a published identity document nor a signer backup on the Homeserver. An unavailable read cannot establish absence. If these checks succeed, the signer keeps its install and policy, chooses a fresh nonce, signs a new `create` carrying the new observation, and atomically replaces the old continuation with the complete new one before sending it. A restart of the process resumes whichever continuation was durably recorded. The ordinary receipt, backup, first delivery and publication rules then apply (spec 4.3 and 12.8). A changed account or a signed version requires the applicable restore or provider-loss procedure.

**Fresh creation evidence.** For a bound `create`, commit to the initial SQL contents before the SQL transaction commits. Keep the nonzero content commitment and its creation with the rollback mark, and the same commitment in the account. Bind the confirmed store, identity, result creation, exact signed enrollment hash, generation, verified initial document hash or absence, all seven announced limits, both directory versions, initial reachability and every permanent exclusion's App ID, reason, time, epoch and rotation status. At fresh creation both directory versions are zero and no reachable app or enrollment-held ID exists. Read permanent rows in raw App ID order within the bound of the signed enrollment. A reservation without the committed SQL contents cannot return an enrollment result. Mark position, service time and key changes retain the commitment's original creation. A different creation cannot use it to authenticate its own state.

For the exact opening `create` receipt at the initial SQL position (no counted write and no sequence), apply Section 3's account/store/erasure checks and verify the initial contents. The initial mark of this same creation must still cover that position, its nonzero commitment must equal the stored commitment, and recomputing it from the locked SQL contents and the exact enrollment must give that value. Otherwise return `503 unavailable` without an accepted head or a receipt mutation. The covered receipt can still be read when the original observation has expired.

A later exact opening receipt must trace its selected SQL history to the same creation commitment. Before the first counted change, verify the actual opening contents against the stored commitment and the unique creating receipt. Carry the resulting origin with the new count and tag. Each further counted change inherits only the origin at its exact preceding count and tag, including across a generation change; later pruning preserves the origin at every retained position. An absent witness stays unproven. For the opening receipt, compare the current origin to the independent creation commitment and, at a positive marked count, check the retained origin and tag at that count and the usual chain verdict. A still-zero mark with a proven descendant can establish only the immutable opening receipt. It supplies no evidence for later exclusions. An unavailable origin or inconsistent history withholds the receipt. Other intents retain their existing receipt rules.

At any count, reconstruct the independent creation commitment using the exact requested enrollment hash, confirmed store, identity, creation, all seven limits that would be returned, and the retained digest of the other initial contents. That digest covers the actual opening generation, verified document, directory and exclusion rows. A receipt lookup or tagged history position alone cannot authenticate these result fields. If any required input differs or is unavailable, return a headless `503 unavailable`. Use the current response signing key under wire 7.4.1.

Before returning the opening receipt, confirm the matching creation in committed SQL under the identity lock and record that confirmation in the same mark. If SQL committed before the confirmation became durable, an exact retry can finish the confirmation after checking the receipt and its history. Preserve the mark's selected position, key and time floor. A confirmed creation identifies the opening only. New exclusions require evidence at the position where the service acknowledged them.

At the opening position, the independent mark may also retain the complete initial document reference, generation and time floors, limits, directory versions, reachability and exclusions, bound to that store and identity. Keep this material together when it fits the 4096-byte mark, including space for later time-floor updates. An opening whose material exceeds this bound requires the corresponding authenticated SQL state. Confirmation and a time-floor change at the same position preserve any complete material already held there.

At a later acknowledged position, the service may retain complete recovery material under the same whole-file bound. For an ordinary counted write that leaves the recovery facts unchanged, the service can carry the material forward from the same store and creation at the locked account's exact count, tag and chain. Before publishing it, the service compares the entire independent predecessor, including the material it held. When a write changes those facts, the service can rebuild them from the verified account under the identity lock. This includes held and permanent exclusions, the prior document, generation and activation, limits, both directory versions and whether any app is reachable. A late completion can fill a mark requiring SQL only at the exact position it committed, with the same key and time floor. A later position keeps its own evidence. If complete material is unavailable or exceeds the bound, the position still requires verified SQL.

### 3.1 Replacing a proven-older account

The takeover may replace an older account only under the identity lock and an unchanged confirmed store, selected mark and erasure judgment. Preserve every known applicable generation bound from the older account's state, its verified document and the selected mark; preserve the verified document/version, stamped-time floor and irrevocable removed/revoked/departed app exclusions. Creation ordering does not imply that the older account's generation, document or stamped time is lower than the mark's: the account can keep a committed generation start while its mark still records zero.

Choose the new creation strictly above both creations and completed erasure, and not below the retained time floor, using usable trusted time. If those requirements cannot be met at or before that trusted time, refuse unavailable; no future time the older account holds is creation authority. Compare and durably update the exact selected mark first, retaining its pair, hash and retirements. Then atomically replace the proven-older account with an empty staged account, its safety baseline, the activation requirement below and the new exact enrollment receipt. No old messages, records, claims, chain, accepted delivery proof or old receipt history is carried. Success follows the replacement's durable commit. A failed or unknown commit leaves the mark and whichever account state actually committed; neither guesses the enrollment outcome.

Every current account persists one required **activation requirement**, `ordinary` or `recovery`, with its account state. It is not inferred from a document's origin/key, a receipt hash, a prior intent or receipt retention. Ordinary new enrollment and a takeover without a superseded account initialize `ordinary` atomically with creation and retain their existing staged/import rules. Replacement retaining a superseded account's baseline initializes `recovery` atomically with that replacement, including beside a zero mark. Both writers preserve the ordinary no-chain/no-delivery-proof distinction: a baseline document is not a delivery accepted in the new lifetime.

While `recovery` is required, the account is staged and recovery-only even if its baseline document names this service. This gate precedes origin/key shortcuts, ordinary serving, equal-document retry recognition and import/activation. Its ordinary requests get the recovery notice, without a head; existing-account enrollment may retain only its normal headless result and exclusions. No import, upload, abort, key change, first-document retry or same-generation document may remove or bypass this requirement. Erasure remains available under its normal authenticated checks and durable-outcome rules. A recovery delivery must have a strictly higher version than every retained document, a generation above every retained account/document/mark floor with `recovery_from` equal to that generation minus one, and all existing signer, key, retirement, exclusion and monotone-time checks.

Only the durable recovery commit that starts that authorized generation may change `recovery` to `ordinary`, in the same commit as the new recovery chain and accepted document/proof. A failed or unknown commit does not authorize a separate clearing write or success; reload uses only the committed account state. A committed recovery whose mark update fails remains under the existing lost-mark/mark-comparison restrictions, releases no head, and cannot be made healthy by the cleared requirement alone. Existing-account enrollment, exact receipt retry, discovery, restart, receipt eviction, imports and ordinary deliveries preserve the requirement. Erasure removes it with the account. No operation sets it by fabricating `recovery_from`, a generation sentinel or an accepted document proof.

### 3.2 Recovering a newer account beside an older mark

The selected account's lifetime keeps its creation, document/version, state generation, stamped time and exclusions. Ordinary reads, enrollment and receipt retry neither replace the older mark nor set its key. A request bound to the older mark conflicts before mutation; an old-mark takeover cannot erase or replace the newer account. Apply the account's existing `mark_lost` restrictions, retaining the older mark's generation as an additional negative recovery bound and every retirement.

An explicit recovery start must exceed every retained account/document/mark generation floor, exceed the held document's version, preserve the exclusions and time floor, and pass the existing recovery checks. After its commit is durable, while the same selected account creation and store identity still hold, atomically compare and replace the **exact older readable mark** with that committed recovery head, its key and the account's creation. Retirements are unchanged. This is the only cross-creation mark replacement for this direction; no ordinary call, enrollment, import, retry or read performs it. A changed old record, account lifetime or store refuses unavailable with no head. A crash or failed replacement after the account's commit leaves the account `mark_lost`; matching the old mark's pair/hash never restores its association. A later authorized recovery still follows all retained floors and the signer's unresolved-start rules.

## 4. Lifecycle and conformance

After a successful creation or takeover, subsequent signer requests bind the returned new creation. Delivery retries are recognized only within that bound account, before document-content retry handling. Thus a first delivery saved from an erased account cannot activate a later staged account even when both accounts use the same service key and document bytes. An old `existing` enrollment cannot recreate an erased account. A new post-erasure creation uses a newly authorized intent and a new authenticated observation of the completed erasure.

The signer's exact pending binding and signed intent survive local reloads and retry ownership changes while the continuation remains active; an enrollment-only continuation ends only as Section 3 permits. A hand-over starts only after prior operations are resolved as its existing rules require.

The authenticated backup or hand-over carries a required non-null `account_created` with `identity_doc`, with exactly three fraction digits as Section 1 requires (wire 3.12-3.13). It binds that exact document to its original account at the document's authenticated service origin, with the document's service-key context. A restore reads this binding before its first signer request and preserves it through original-document replay and subsequent delivery, including the existing first-document, `prev` and mid-recovery paths. The hand-over receiver likewise uses it for its signer reads, take-over delivery and retries. A recovery with the key alone binds instead the creation of Section 3's discovery, as spec 12.8 says. All existing pin, copy, version, generation, signer and recovery checks still apply, including the old serving-key check on a restore. The carrier proves neither service acceptance nor the outcome of an earlier pending operation. Discovery cannot replace this target with the currently hosted account; it may support a distinct authorized action under Section 3 while earlier unknown outcomes remain unresolved.

The unchanged erasure rules retain the original authorized body/binding during unknown outcomes, permit their specified authenticated document conflict handling at the same creation, and never retarget a pending erasure to a newly discovered later creation.

Conformance covers strict field presence and signed bytes, ordinary same-account success/retry, both cross-creation replays, lock acquisition after erasure, exact receipt recovery, receipt eviction and unknown outcomes, fresh post-erasure creation, marked lost-account takeover, discovery at each applicable verdict, store replacement and unresolved erasure, and validity immediately before/at/after expiry and with future/unusable trusted time. Conformance also covers required backup/hand-over creation fields, their exact grammar and document association, original-account replay through the first-document, `prev` and mid-recovery paths, and refusal to substitute a different creation. Enrollment continuation cases cover exact retries across reloads, explicit authorized termination with the remote outcome still unknown, and a late answer that cannot advance the replacement. Generated signed cases include altered creation, origin, identity, store identity, erased time, observation time/expiry, original request fields, response status/head and signatures; each rejection isolates its own rule. Signed cases are generated and checked byte for byte; no signed fixture bytes are edited by hand.

Association conformance covers equality, both creation orders, missing versus unreadable marks, selected response keys and all retained floors. It includes a higher-generation/document/time account baseline beside a zero mark; required signed association presence, exact values and created/null consistency; exact pending-intent preservation; same-account receipt retry and refusal of a superseded account's receipt; and the distinct erasure paths that delete a present account. Activation controls distinguish ordinary move-target enrollment from replacement with the same staged document, exercise restart and receipt eviction, and refuse origin/key, equal-document, import and activation bypasses before a strictly higher recovery succeeds. Crash controls cover the takeover mark before the account's replacement, committed replacement with a lost answer, durable recovery with failed mark replacement, changed store/record before replacement, and failed or unknown erasure deletion. These signed cases are also generated and checked by strict consumers.

Observation-size conformance covers the maximum allowed origin and one-byte excess, domain and label bounds, the maximum compact field set, exact signed bodies at 1,024 and 1,025 bytes, base64 length before decoding, and preserved noncanonical signed bytes. It also covers the complete maximum Create/Takeover envelope at and above 256 KiB with revoked-app accounting, and unchanged byte-for-byte pending retries.
